## Purpose

Define the measurable objectives by which the initial NuBlox project will be judged and distinguish delivery outputs from actual project success.

## Objective 1 — Validate the customer problem

Success evidence:

- representative customer research completed;
- priority operational pain points evidenced rather than assumed;
- target segment confirmed, narrowed or changed through a controlled decision;
- willingness-to-change conditions documented;
- priority end-to-end workflows selected from evidence.

## Objective 2 — Establish a requirements baseline

Success evidence:

- business requirements documented;
- functional and non-functional requirements traceable to customer/business outcomes;
- data, integration and reporting requirements established;
- business rules and acceptance criteria defined;
- unresolved requirements are visible and owned.

## Objective 3 — Establish a coherent architecture

Success evidence:

- approved system context and solution architecture;
- clear responsibility boundaries;
- security/privacy/data/integration/migration architecture defined;
- material architecture decisions recorded with rationale;
- architecture traceable to approved requirements;
- major technical risks validated through spikes/prototypes where necessary.

## Objective 4 — Prove end-to-end product execution

Success evidence:

- at least one representative end-to-end business outcome operates through production-quality foundations;
- business information remains coherent across the workflow;
- security, permissions, decisions and audit evidence behave as required;
- failure/recovery scenarios are tested;
- automated tests protect critical behaviour.

## Objective 5 — Demonstrate acceptable product quality

Success evidence shall include approved thresholds for:

- functional correctness;
- security;
- data integrity;
- reliability/recovery;
- performance;
- accessibility;
- usability;
- maintainability;
- observability/supportability.

Threshold values are not invented in this draft; they will be set through requirements and test planning.

## Objective 6 — Demonstrate customer value

Success evidence:

- pre-pilot baselines agreed;
- pilot workflows measurably improve one or more agreed outcomes;
- adoption/user feedback supports continued use;
- material disbenefits are identified and understood;
- customer sponsor judges value sufficient to continue or expand.

## Objective 7 — Demonstrate repeatable implementation

Success evidence:

- configuration does not require a customer-specific code fork;
- migration/onboarding steps are documented and repeatable;
- integration patterns are reusable;
- support burden is measurable and supportable;
- implementation effort is sufficiently predictable for commercial modelling.

## Objective 8 — Demonstrate commercial plausibility

Success evidence:

- costed product/service model exists;
- pricing hypothesis exists;
- customer TCO/value case can be explained;
- provider TCO and unit economics can be modelled;
- investment case can be updated with evidence rather than speculation.

## Objective 9 — Establish production readiness

Success evidence:

- operational readiness review completed;
- monitoring, incident, backup/recovery and support procedures exist;
- security/performance evidence is acceptable;
- release/cutover/rollback controls exist;
- known residual risks are accepted by the relevant authority.

## Non-success conditions

The project is not successful merely because:

- code has been written;
- many features exist;
- documents are complete;
- a demo works on happy paths;
- a pilot starts;
- a customer signs without successful adoption/value.

## Measurement governance

Each success criterion shall eventually have:

- metric/definition;
- baseline;
- target/threshold;
- evidence source;
- owner;
- measurement cadence;
- acceptance authority.
