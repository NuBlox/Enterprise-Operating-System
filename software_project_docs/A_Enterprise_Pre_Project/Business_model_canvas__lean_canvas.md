## Purpose

Capture the current NuBlox business-model hypotheses in one place so product, market, commercial and delivery assumptions can be tested explicitly.

## Customer segments

### Initial hypothesis

UK mid-market multidisciplinary built-environment consultancies and project-services organisations with meaningful cross-functional complexity and fragmented operational tooling.

### Expansion hypotheses

- main contractors;
- specialist contractors;
- developers/owners;
- asset and facilities operators;
- integrated engineering/construction organisations;
- other project-, service- or asset-intensive sectors where the operating/delivery divide is material.

No segment is approved until supported by customer evidence.

## Customer problems

Hypotheses to validate:

- fragmented enterprise and delivery systems;
- duplicate information and reconciliation;
- weak handoffs between winning, planning, delivering and commercialising work;
- disconnected professional outputs and business controls;
- delayed or reconstructed management information;
- unclear operational context across teams and systems;
- high cost of integrations and local spreadsheet/email workarounds.

## Value proposition

Enable organisations to operate the business and deliver the work through one coherent, governed digital environment, reducing fragmentation while retaining the specialist depth needed for real work.

## Solution hypothesis

NuBlox should progressively provide or coordinate the capabilities required to connect:

```text
market/client need
→ commercial commitment
→ organisation/resources
→ delivery work
→ information/work products
→ decisions/controls
→ commercial/financial consequences
→ evidence/performance
→ next business outcome
```

This is a strategic flow, not a fixed software architecture.

## Channels

Candidate channels to validate:

- founder/direct enterprise sales;
- direct consultative sales;
- industry networks and professional communities;
- implementation/advisory partners;
- digital inbound and thought leadership;
- pilot/reference-customer introductions.

## Customer relationships

Early customers are expected to require high-touch discovery, onboarding and implementation. The target long-term model should increase repeatability and reduce customer-specific engineering.

## Revenue streams

Hypotheses only:

- recurring software subscription;
- implementation/migration services;
- premium support or service tiers;
- selected platform/integration services;
- possible usage or scale-based components where aligned to customer value.

Final pricing and packaging are not decided here.

## Key activities

- product/domain research;
- software product development;
- security/compliance assurance;
- customer discovery;
- implementation/migration capability;
- support/customer success;
- integration/ecosystem development;
- product operations and continuous improvement.

## Key resources

- product and domain knowledge;
- engineering capability;
- secure cloud/platform capability;
- controlled product/architecture documentation;
- customer evidence;
- implementation patterns;
- brand and market credibility;
- partner/specialist expertise where necessary.

## Key partners

Potential partners include:

- cloud/platform providers;
- security and assurance specialists;
- legal/privacy advisers;
- implementation/integration partners;
- specialist software vendors whose capabilities are deliberately retained;
- professional/industry bodies;
- design, engineering and construction organisations participating in discovery and pilots.

## Cost structure

Key cost drivers are defined in the controlled TCO and budget documents and include product/engineering, cloud operations, security/compliance, customer implementation, support, sales/marketing and professional services.

## Unfair advantage / defensibility hypotheses

Potential defensibility must be earned through:

- a coherent enterprise-and-delivery semantic model;
- deep understanding of real professional/project work;
- repeatable customer configuration without product forks;
- trusted historical/audit context;
- migration/integration competence;
- accumulated workflow and operating-model knowledge;
- high switching value created by genuine operational coherence.

None of these is yet proven.

## Critical assumptions to test

1. The target customers experience the proposed fragmentation severely enough to pay to solve it.
2. They will trust a new platform with sufficiently important operational work.
3. NuBlox can provide meaningful depth without becoming bespoke software.
4. Migration and change costs do not overwhelm customer value.
5. A recurring software model can achieve viable gross margins despite enterprise implementation needs.
6. NuBlox can coexist with specialist tools without losing the coherence that defines its value proposition.
