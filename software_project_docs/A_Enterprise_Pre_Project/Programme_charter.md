## Purpose

Establish the NuBlox Enterprise Operating System as a controlled programme of product discovery, software development, market validation and operational readiness, with defined objectives, governance principles, decision gates and scope boundaries.

## Programme vision

Create an enterprise operating system that can enable organisations to operate the business and deliver the work through which they create value in one coherent, governed digital environment.

## Programme objectives

The programme shall:

1. validate the customer problem and initial target segment;
2. define the product outcomes and business requirements;
3. establish a coherent, secure and maintainable solution architecture;
4. build and verify the product through controlled end-to-end slices;
5. prove measurable customer value through pilots;
6. establish repeatable implementation, migration, support and operations;
7. prepare the product for commercial production use;
8. preserve traceability from strategy through requirements, decisions, code, tests, release and realised benefits.

## Programme scope

### In scope

- product and market discovery;
- customer research;
- commercial/business case development;
- requirements and business analysis;
- product/UX design;
- solution/data/security/integration architecture;
- software engineering;
- testing and assurance;
- migration/integration capability;
- deployment and operations;
- customer onboarding and support model;
- documentation and controlled governance;
- launch and subsequent product expansion.

### Not authorised by this charter version

- an unrestricted feature catalogue;
- a fixed technology stack;
- a final architecture;
- full-scale engineering spend;
- expansion into every industry or jurisdiction;
- customer-specific product forks;
- claims of ROI or market fit without evidence.

## Programme workstreams

1. **Product & Market** — vision, customer research, product management, roadmap and commercial proposition.
2. **Business Analysis & Requirements** — end-to-end workflows, requirements, rules, data and acceptance.
3. **Architecture & Engineering** — solution design, software delivery, technical quality and maintainability.
4. **Data, Migration & Integration** — information architecture, migration, interoperability and data governance.
5. **Security, Privacy & Compliance** — secure-by-design engineering, assurance, privacy and regulatory controls.
6. **UX & Adoption** — user research, interaction design, accessibility, training and change.
7. **Quality & Assurance** — test strategy, traceability, performance, security and acceptance.
8. **Operations & Service** — deployment, monitoring, continuity, support and service management.
9. **Commercial & Customer Success** — pricing, contracts, implementation, pilots, support and benefits evidence.

## Governance principles

- Decisions are made at the lowest appropriate level, with material irreversible decisions escalated.
- Approved requirements and controlled architecture decisions govern implementation.
- Risk, security, privacy and compliance are integrated into normal delivery.
- Customer evidence can overturn internal assumptions.
- Material scope, budget or architecture changes require traceable decision records.
- `main` is the authoritative repository branch for completed programme work; residual working branches/PRs are not considered complete delivery.

## Major decision gates

### Gate 0 — Pre-project evidence

Required: product/market hypothesis, customer evidence plan, business case, feasibility, risks, funding approach and charter.

### Gate 1 — Project initiation

Required: project charter/PID, governance, scope, objectives, stakeholder baseline, assumptions/constraints and initial plan.

### Gate 2 — Requirements baseline

Required: prioritised requirements, end-to-end scenarios, non-functional requirements, traceability and acceptance approach.

### Gate 3 — Architecture baseline

Required: solution architecture, security/privacy architecture, data/integration/migration design and material ADRs.

### Gate 4 — Foundation proof

Required: production-quality foundation and at least one representative end-to-end slice proving architecture, quality and deployment approach.

### Gate 5 — Pilot readiness

Required: coherent pilot scope, migration/onboarding controls, service readiness, test evidence and measurable success criteria.

### Gate 6 — Production launch

Required: acceptance, security/performance evidence, operational readiness, support, release/cutover controls and approved commercial launch.

## Programme success measures

Success measures will be refined through requirements and benefit baselines. The programme should ultimately demonstrate:

- validated customer problem/market fit;
- measurable reduction in targeted operational fragmentation;
- successful completion of representative end-to-end business workflows;
- secure, resilient and maintainable operation;
- repeatable customer onboarding/configuration/migration;
- measurable customer outcomes;
- commercially viable unit economics;
- controlled product evolution without customer-specific fragmentation.

## Roles and authorities

Named roles are not yet fully appointed. The Project Initiation phase shall establish at minimum:

- Programme Sponsor / investment authority;
- Product Owner / product decision authority;
- Programme/Project Manager;
- Architecture authority;
- Engineering lead;
- Security/privacy authority;
- Quality/acceptance authority;
- Commercial/customer authority;
- Data governance authority.

No placeholder role should be treated as appointed until explicitly confirmed.

## Principal programme risks

The controlled Enterprise Risk Register remains authoritative for risk detail. Principal themes include:

- insufficient validated customer pain;
- excessive scope breadth;
- non-repeatable customer variation;
- migration/integration complexity;
- security/compliance shortfall;
- funding constraints;
- incumbent competitive response;
- architecture complexity and technical debt;
- operational/support burden;
- insufficient adoption/change capability.

## Programme reporting

The formal Project Initiation phase shall establish reporting cadence and controls. Expected reporting includes:

- decision log;
- RAID status;
- budget/forecast;
- roadmap/milestones;
- requirements/architecture status;
- engineering/quality evidence;
- customer research/pilot outcomes;
- benefit evidence;
- security/compliance status.

## Charter status

This Draft v0.1 establishes the proposed programme structure but does not itself authorise expenditure or appoint named authorities. Those actions require approval through the appropriate funding and governance records.
