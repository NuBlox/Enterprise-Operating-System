## Purpose

Identify the principal competitor groups relevant to NuBlox, document their current positioning and strengths, and define where NuBlox must create demonstrably different customer value.

## Competitive categories

NuBlox is likely to face competition from several overlapping categories rather than one direct peer group.

### Enterprise suite vendors

Representative vendors:

- SAP;
- Oracle;
- Microsoft Dynamics 365;
- Workday;
- IFS;
- Infor.

These vendors offer broad enterprise capabilities and increasingly position around connected data, automation, AI, industry solutions and end-to-end business processes.

### Workflow / service / customer platforms

Representative vendors:

- ServiceNow;
- Salesforce.

These platforms compete through configurable workflow, customer/service operations, platform extensibility, automation and ecosystem strength.

### Professional-services / project ERP

Representative vendors:

- Deltek Vantagepoint;
- Unanet.

These vendors are particularly relevant to the current built-environment consultancy beachhead hypothesis because they combine project operations, resource planning, client pipeline and financial management for project-based professional-services firms.

### Construction / project-delivery platforms

Representative vendors:

- Procore;
- Autodesk Construction Cloud / Autodesk Forma;
- Oracle Aconex;
- Oracle Primavera Unifier;
- Trimble / Viewpoint.

These platforms provide deep project-delivery, field, information-management, cost and collaboration capability, frequently alongside or integrated with separate ERP/accounting systems.

## Competitor observations

| Competitor / category | Current strength relevant to NuBlox | Strategic implication for NuBlox |
|---|---|---|
| SAP | Broad enterprise process coverage, installed base, finance/supply-chain depth, ecosystem | NuBlox cannot rely on generic ERP breadth as differentiation |
| Oracle Fusion | Broad ERP, project, procurement, risk, SCM, HCM, sales/service coverage | Cross-functional scope is already established in market expectations |
| Microsoft Dynamics 365 | Connected ERP/CRM portfolio, Microsoft ecosystem, flexible adoption | “Use one or several connected apps” is already a strong mainstream proposition |
| Workday | Strong HCM/finance enterprise management positioning | People/finance coherence alone is insufficient differentiation |
| IFS | Strong asset/service/construction/engineering industry depth | Industry-specific enterprise execution is already a credible incumbent strategy |
| Infor | Industry-focused ERP suites and cloud positioning | Verticalisation must be deeper than marketing labels |
| Salesforce | Customer platform, ecosystem, extensibility, automation | CRM/customer workflow cannot be treated as an isolated NuBlox differentiator |
| ServiceNow | Workflow/platform model and enterprise service management | Configurable enterprise workflow is table stakes |

## Key competitive conclusion

NuBlox should assume that competitors can already match many generic product claims:

- cloud;
- AI/agents;
- workflow;
- configurable applications;
- dashboards;
- mobile access;
- APIs/integration;
- unified data;
- industry-specific solutions;
- end-to-end process language.

Therefore the competitive question is not **whether NuBlox can contain comparable features**. It is whether NuBlox can produce a better end-to-end operating outcome with less fragmentation and lower organisational friction.

## Differentiation hypotheses to test

### 1. Enterprise-to-delivery continuity

Can NuBlox keep business development, contract/appointment, resource planning, professional/project delivery, controlled outputs, decisions, commercial performance, billing and management information connected without the customer assembling that continuity across multiple products?

### 2. Work-centred rather than module-centred experience

Can a user operate from the work they are responsible for without needing to understand which enterprise module owns each piece of the process?

### 3. Professional outputs as part of business execution

Can NuBlox connect drawings, models, reports, surveys, specifications, schedules and other professional outputs to the commercial, project and governance context that caused them to exist?

### 4. Embedded governance

Can approvals, responsibilities, evidence and assurance be captured as a natural consequence of performing work rather than as separate administrative processes?

### 5. Selective specialist-tool coexistence

Can NuBlox preserve business continuity while allowing specialist authoring environments to remain in use where they are genuinely best-of-breed?

## Competitive risks

- incumbent suites may extend into the same whitespace before NuBlox reaches market;
- customers may prefer integration between established tools over platform replacement;
- migration cost may outweigh the theoretical benefit of consolidation;
- specialist depth may be difficult to reproduce economically;
- large vendors have stronger ecosystems, channels, certifications and enterprise trust;
- consultancy buyers may see Deltek/Unanet as sufficient unless NuBlox demonstrates deeper delivery value;
- construction buyers may see Procore/Autodesk/Oracle/Trimble as sufficient unless NuBlox demonstrates enterprise continuity that materially changes economics or control.

## Competitive validation required

Before approving the product strategy, NuBlox should complete scenario-based comparisons rather than feature checklists. Representative scenarios should include:

1. opportunity-to-engagement-to-cash;
2. project mobilisation and resource assignment;
3. production, review and issue of a professional deliverable;
4. project change with commercial and programme consequence;
5. supplier/subconsultant engagement;
6. management visibility from operational work;
7. project closeout and knowledge/record retention.

For each scenario, compare:

- number of systems required;
- number of manual handoffs;
- duplicate data entry;
- workflow continuity;
- decision/evidence traceability;
- configuration complexity;
- implementation burden;
- user experience;
- total operating cost.
