## Purpose

Set out the current investment rationale for the NuBlox Enterprise Operating System, the benefits that must be proven, the principal costs and risks, and the evidence required before a formal investment decision can be made.

## Executive summary

NuBlox is intended to address operational fragmentation between enterprise management systems and the actual professional, project and operational work through which organisations deliver value.

The investment thesis is that a coherent enterprise operating environment could reduce duplicate work, manual reconciliation, broken handoffs and weak traceability while improving management visibility, governance and execution quality.

At version 0.1, the case is **not yet financially approved**. Market, customer, feasibility, cost and revenue assumptions remain to be validated.

## Strategic case

### Problem

Organisations may operate through combinations of ERP, CRM, HCM, project systems, specialist industry software, document repositories, spreadsheets and email. These systems often optimise particular functions but may leave the complete business outcome fragmented across system boundaries.

### Opportunity

NuBlox may create value by connecting:

- organisational context;
- customer and supplier relationships;
- work and delivery;
- business information and professional outputs;
- commercial/financial consequences;
- approvals, decisions and evidence;
- management information.

### Strategic fit

The programme aligns with a product vision of enabling an organisation to operate the business and deliver the work it exists to perform in one coherent digital environment.

## Economic case

The economic case must compare the costs and benefits of at least these options:

1. **Do nothing / continue current system estate** — customers retain existing products, integrations and manual processes.
2. **Integration-led improvement** — customers keep incumbent systems and improve integration, workflow and data architecture.
3. **NuBlox as a focused operating layer** — NuBlox coordinates selected end-to-end workflows while incumbent transactional/specialist systems remain.
4. **NuBlox as broader enterprise operating system** — NuBlox progressively replaces or consolidates more of the existing application estate.

The preferred option should be determined by evidence rather than by product ambition alone.

## Benefit hypotheses

Potential customer benefits to quantify include:

- reduced licence and integration complexity where systems can be consolidated;
- reduced duplicate data entry;
- reduced manual reconciliation;
- shorter project/work handoff times;
- faster mobilisation from opportunity/contract to delivery;
- improved utilisation and resource planning;
- reduced project write-offs and commercial leakage;
- faster, more accurate billing;
- improved cash visibility;
- reduced rework caused by information/control failures;
- improved traceability of decisions, approvals and deliverables;
- faster management reporting;
- improved compliance and assurance evidence;
- reduced onboarding/training burden caused by fragmented applications.

None of these benefits should be included in an approved financial model until a baseline and measurement method exist.

## Cost categories

Programme costs are expected to include:

- product management and domain research;
- software engineering;
- UX/design;
- quality assurance;
- cloud infrastructure and environments;
- security engineering and independent testing;
- compliance/legal/privacy work;
- data migration tooling;
- integration development;
- customer onboarding and implementation;
- support and operations;
- documentation and training;
- sales, marketing and customer acquisition;
- third-party services, licences and specialist advice.

Detailed costs are not yet estimated in this version.

## Commercial case

Commercial assumptions to validate include:

- SaaS/subscription delivery may be the primary model, but deployment options are not yet approved;
- pricing should reflect customer value and operating scale rather than arbitrary feature fragmentation;
- implementation/migration services may require separate commercial treatment;
- initial customer acquisition is likely to require high-touch discovery and onboarding;
- references and measurable outcomes from early customers will be critical to enterprise credibility.

A pricing strategy and financial model are required before investment approval.

## Management case

The programme should be governed through staged evidence gates:

1. product vision and market hypothesis;
2. customer discovery and target-segment validation;
3. feasibility and risk assessment;
4. requirements and solution options;
5. prototype/pilot evidence;
6. business-case update with validated costs/benefits;
7. controlled implementation and release.

The programme should avoid committing to large-scale implementation before the preceding evidence gates are sufficiently mature.

## Options appraisal criteria

Each strategic option should be assessed against:

- customer value;
- differentiation;
- implementation complexity;
- migration difficulty;
- security/regulatory burden;
- time to market;
- product scalability;
- recurring revenue potential;
- gross-margin potential;
- support burden;
- partner/ecosystem requirements;
- risk of customer-specific customisation.

## Principal risks

- insufficient customer pain to justify switching;
- target segment too narrow to support growth;
- scope too broad to deliver economically;
- incumbent vendors close the identified gap;
- implementation becomes consultancy-heavy and non-repeatable;
- specialist tools remain indispensable, limiting consolidation value;
- data migration and integration costs overwhelm benefits;
- security/compliance expectations delay market entry;
- product fails to achieve sufficiently differentiated user experience;
- development cost exceeds available funding.

## Investment decision status

**No formal investment approval is made by this draft.**

Before approval, the programme requires at minimum:

- customer-discovery evidence;
- initial market sizing;
- feasibility assessment;
- cost-benefit model;
- delivery/resource estimate;
- funding model;
- commercial/pricing hypothesis;
- risk register;
- initial product roadmap;
- measurable pilot success criteria.

## Recommendation at version 0.1

Continue controlled pre-project discovery and feasibility work. Do not treat the current vision as sufficient authority for full-scale software implementation.
