## Purpose

Define how the programme will evaluate whether NuBlox creates sufficient economic value to justify investment by NuBlox and adoption by customers.

## Current status

No approved ROI, NPV, IRR or payback claim exists at version 0.1. This analysis defines the cost/benefit categories, baselines and measurement approach required before financial conclusions are made.

## Evaluation perspectives

The cost-benefit model must be evaluated from two perspectives:

1. **Customer economics** — whether buying and implementing NuBlox creates net value compared with the customer's current operating model and alternatives.
2. **NuBlox economics** — whether building, selling, onboarding, operating and supporting the product can produce a sustainable business.

## Customer baseline cost categories

A representative customer baseline should quantify, where applicable:

- ERP/accounting licences and support;
- CRM licences;
- project-management/project-controls licences;
- resource/time systems;
- document/information-management systems;
- workflow/automation tools;
- integration platform and connector costs;
- specialist implementation/support costs;
- manual administration and reconciliation effort;
- spreadsheet/email workarounds;
- duplicated data maintenance;
- reporting consolidation effort;
- change/rework caused by handoff or information failures;
- project write-offs and revenue leakage;
- delayed billing and cash collection;
- audit/compliance evidence preparation;
- training and onboarding across multiple applications.

## Customer NuBlox cost categories

Potential customer costs include:

- subscription/licence charges;
- implementation/configuration;
- data migration;
- integrations that remain necessary;
- training/change management;
- temporary dual-running;
- internal programme/resource costs;
- support/services;
- process redesign;
- exit/switching cost if the customer later leaves NuBlox.

## Customer benefit categories

Potential measurable benefits include:

### Productivity

- reduced duplicate entry;
- reduced manual reconciliation;
- fewer application switches;
- reduced administrative effort;
- faster information retrieval;
- faster handoffs and approvals.

### Project/commercial performance

- improved utilisation;
- improved resource allocation;
- reduced write-offs;
- reduced fee leakage;
- faster change recognition;
- improved forecast accuracy;
- faster billing;
- improved cash conversion.

### Quality and governance

- reduced rework;
- improved review/approval traceability;
- more reliable records/evidence;
- fewer uncontrolled spreadsheets/processes;
- reduced audit preparation effort;
- improved compliance visibility.

### Management effectiveness

- faster access to current operating information;
- less management-report assembly;
- earlier exception detection;
- stronger connection between delivery and financial performance.

### Technology estate

- avoided licences where systems can be retired;
- reduced integration count;
- reduced support/vendor overhead;
- simpler identity/access administration;
- lower change cost where one controlled change can replace several system changes.

## Benefit evidence standard

A benefit should not enter the approved financial model unless it has:

- a defined baseline;
- a defined target state;
- a measurement method;
- an accountable owner;
- a period over which it will be measured;
- evidence supporting the expected improvement;
- treatment of displacement or implementation cost.

Potential revenue streams to evaluate include:

- recurring platform subscription;
- user/usage-based subscription components where justified;
- implementation/onboarding services;
- migration services;
- premium support;
- specialist/industry solution packages only where they create separately defensible value;
- partner/channel revenue opportunities.

No pricing model is approved by this document.

## Scenarios to model

At minimum model:

1. **Conservative case** — slower customer acquisition, higher implementation cost, lower consolidation benefit.
2. **Expected case** — evidence-based adoption and delivery assumptions.
3. **Upside case** — stronger repeatability, faster implementation and higher expansion.
4. **Downside/stress case** — delayed launch, higher engineering/support cost and weak conversion.

## Required financial outputs

The next financial model should calculate:

- customer payback period;
- customer five-year TCO delta;
- customer net benefit;
- NuBlox development and operating cash requirement;
- gross margin;
- customer acquisition cost;
- implementation gross margin;
- annual recurring revenue;
- retention/expansion assumptions;
- cash runway;
- NPV/IRR/payback where sufficient data exists.

## Current conclusion

A cost-benefit case is **plausible but unproven**. The programme should not use hypothetical productivity percentages or invented licence savings as evidence. Primary customer baselines and actual delivery-cost estimates are required.
