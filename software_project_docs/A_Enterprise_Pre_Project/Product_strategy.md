## Purpose

Define how NuBlox should pursue the product vision, what strategic hypotheses must be proven, which market entry path appears most credible, and what choices must remain open until further evidence exists.

## Strategic thesis

NuBlox should compete by reducing the operational gap between **running an organisation** and **delivering the work through which that organisation creates value**.

The strategic opportunity is not simply to place ERP, CRM, HCM, projects and documents under one menu. Established vendors already provide broad suites. NuBlox must instead prove that customers gain material value when operational, professional, project, commercial and governance work remains connected through the full business outcome.

## Working market-entry hypothesis

The first launch segment should be tested among **UK mid-market multidisciplinary built-environment consultancies and project-services firms**, particularly organisations with several of the following characteristics:

- approximately 50–500 employees;
- project-based or assignment-based revenue;
- multidisciplinary professional services;
- meaningful sales, resource, project, commercial and finance processes;
- substantial production and control of professional outputs such as drawings, reports, surveys, specifications, schedules, models or advice;
- collaboration with clients, contractors, specialists and suppliers;
- fragmented use of ERP/accounting, CRM, project management, document management and specialist authoring systems.

This is a **hypothesis to validate**, not an approved permanent market boundary.

## Why this segment is attractive for discovery

A multidisciplinary consultancy offers enough complexity to test the NuBlox proposition across the whole business while avoiding the full first-release burden of plant, manufacturing, warehousing, heavy site labour, logistics and subcontract execution.

It also creates a demanding test of whether NuBlox can connect:

```text
market opportunity
→ proposal / fee
→ appointment / contract
→ project mobilisation
→ resource planning
→ professional delivery
→ controlled outputs
→ review / decision / issue
→ commercial and financial control
→ billing / cash
→ performance and client relationship
```

If NuBlox cannot create a meaningfully better experience across that chain than incumbent project ERP plus specialist tools, the strategy should be reconsidered.

## Strategic differentiation to prove

NuBlox should investigate whether it can create defensible differentiation through the combination of:

1. **End-to-end outcome continuity** — keeping upstream commercial intent, delivery work, outputs, decisions and downstream financial consequences connected.
2. **Work-centred user experience** — showing people the work they need to perform, rather than forcing them to navigate software-module boundaries.
3. **Enterprise and professional delivery in one operating context** — connecting corporate operations to project/professional execution without flattening specialist depth.
4. **Governance embedded in execution** — responsibilities, approvals, evidence and assurance captured through normal work.
5. **Configurable depth without product fragmentation** — supporting different organisations and industries without creating unmaintainable customer-specific forks.
6. **Deliberate coexistence with specialist tools** — integrating or governing outputs where specialist authoring environments remain the sensible place to work.

## Strategic non-differentiators

The following should not be treated as sufficient differentiation on their own because they are already common market claims:

- cloud delivery;
- AI or agents;
- dashboards and analytics;
- workflow automation;
- a single source of truth;
- configurable applications;
- mobile access;
- integrations and APIs;
- industry templates;
- unified data.

NuBlox may require all of these, but competitive advantage must come from how they combine to improve real end-to-end business outcomes.

## Product scope strategy

### Horizon 1 — prove the operating model

Prove that one target customer can run a meaningful cross-section of its business and delivery work through NuBlox with measurable reduction in fragmentation, manual reconciliation and handoff failure.

### Horizon 2 — deepen the launch segment

Expand professional, project, commercial, financial, people and information-management depth until NuBlox can support the normal operating model of the chosen segment without excessive external process workarounds.

### Horizon 3 — expand adjacently

Extend to adjacent built-environment segments such as specialist contractors, main contractors, developers/owners and asset operators only after identifying which capabilities genuinely generalise and which require segment-specific depth.

### Horizon 4 — evaluate broader industries

Only after the product model proves reusable beyond the initial industry should NuBlox consider additional sectors.

## Product boundary principles

For each candidate capability, NuBlox should decide whether to:

- perform the work natively;
- coordinate an external service/system;
- govern the inputs, outputs and lifecycle while specialist authoring remains external;
- exclude the capability from the product boundary.

The decision should be based on customer value, control needs, differentiation, cost, implementation complexity and the maturity of specialist alternatives.

## Adoption strategy hypotheses

Initial adoption should favour a sequence that can demonstrate value without requiring immediate whole-enterprise replacement. Possible entry points to test include:

- project/engagement mobilisation and delivery control;
- commercial and resource management around professional projects;
- controlled professional outputs and approvals;
- opportunity-to-project continuity;
- project-to-billing and performance continuity.

The preferred entry path should be selected through customer discovery and feasibility analysis, not assumed here.

## Strategic success tests

The strategy should progress only if discovery can demonstrate evidence of:

- material customer pain caused by fragmented operating systems and handoffs;
- willingness to change existing processes or systems;
- a buyer with authority and budget to sponsor the change;
- measurable value from reduced duplication, faster handoffs, improved control or improved margin/cash/performance visibility;
- a repeatable operating pattern across more than one customer;
- a credible implementation path that does not require excessive bespoke consulting;
- a realistic commercial model capable of funding product development and support.

## Principal strategy risks

- scope becoming too broad before a repeatable beachhead is proven;
- building a generic platform with insufficient professional depth;
- reproducing incumbent suite features without creating a stronger workflow outcome;
- underestimating data migration and organisational change;
- attempting to replace specialist authoring tools where integration would create more value;
- customer-specific configuration causing product fragmentation;
- entering a segment with deeply entrenched incumbents but insufficient switching value;
- allowing architecture decisions to precede validated product requirements.

## Decisions required next

1. Validate or reject the initial customer-segment hypothesis.
2. Complete structured market sizing and buyer analysis.
3. Conduct primary customer discovery.
4. Define the first repeatable business outcomes to solve.
5. Create business case, feasibility and cost-benefit analyses.
6. Define product roadmap only after the first four steps have credible evidence.
